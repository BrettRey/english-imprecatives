\begin{abstract}
English subjectless imprecatives and benedictives~-- expressions without an overt subject where an expressive head combines directly with an NP (\mention{Damn the bell!}, \mention{Screw you for putting me in this position!}, \mention{Bless his little cotton socks!})~-- pair obligatory accusative pronouns and open lexical NPs with defective verbal syntax. Previous accounts rely mostly on introspection over \mention{damn} and \mention{fuck}. This comparative corpus study maps thirteen core heads, six \mention{fuck}-euphemisms, and archaic \mention{confound} across 19.95~million deduplicated words of locally audited corpora (CABNC, BNC Baby XML, OANC), 11.83~million words of contemporary unscripted British speech (Spoken BNC2014), and a 2.225-billion-word subtitle and web search space (TV Corpus, GloWbE), plus an exploratory COCA check. Four empirical findings organize the family. First, open lexical-NP complementation separates these heads from six primary interjections (0/148 local; 0/53 TV candidate strings) and pronoun-restricted \mention{yay}/\mention{boo} (0/13 TV lexical-NP and 0/17 Spoken BNC2014 hits), while non-reflexive \mention{you} and \mention{me} rule out imperatives and speaker-subject reduced performatives. Second, in the primary PP comparison ($N = 82$ shared-pronoun audited tokens), \mention{to hell} is 97.0\% \mention{damn}-family (32/33; undisplayed-hit bounds 97.0--98.1\%) whereas evaluative \mention{for V-ing} is 24.5\% \mention{damn}-family (12/49; bounds 16.2--27.5\%; minimum gap 69.5 percentage points), spanning six heads including secular \mention{fuck}/\mention{screw} (37 subjectless tokens, joining retaliatory \mention{fuck up}/\mention{screw over} and praise/blame frames). Third, overt \mention{God} concentrates on theological heads (15/15 verified Spoken BNC2014 optatives; both \mention{god fuck} hits are standalone \mention{oh [my] god} exclamations) and specific civic \mention{bless} phrases. Fourth, partial euphemistic correspondence replicates across \mention{damn}- and \mention{fuck}-variants in subtitles alongside sharp limits (39/39 matched-target \mention{God dang/darn} on \mention{it}; 1 nominal \mention{Rules be darned} vs.\ 167 \mention{be damned/buggered} tokens; 0/11 \mention{fuck}-euphemisms in Spoken BNC2014). Together, these findings define the empirical profile lexicalist, constructional, and interjection-phrase accounts need to capture.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} imprecatives, benedictives, construction grammar, defective verbs, English syntax

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ===========================================================================

English contains a pragmatically vivid family of expressions without an overt grammatical subject in which an expressive head combines directly with a noun phrase (which may itself be determined, possessive, or pronominal) and, optionally, a prepositional phrase. Attested examples span secular taboo maledictives, theological curses, affectionate benedictives, and mild minced oaths, as illustrated in (\ref{ex:intro-paradigm}).

\ea\label{ex:intro-paradigm}
\ea Damn the bell! \hfill (BNC Baby \texttt{H85})
\ex Fuck the doctors. \hfill (BNC Baby \texttt{CCW})
\ex Sod the choir! \hfill (BNC Baby \texttt{KPU})
\ex Bugger the rest! \hfill (CABNC \texttt{KB7})
\ex Screw the Cabinet and the rest. \hfill (OANC \texttt{Slate})
\ex Bless you for helping me out with the Watsons. \hfill (BNC Baby \texttt{G0Y})
\ex Oh, darn the luck. \hfill (TV Corpus, \emph{The Simpsons})
\z
\z

Since \textcite[3--7]{quang1971} analysed \mention{fuck you} and \mention{damn him} as \enquote{English sentences without overt grammatical subject} headed by \enquote{quasi-verbs}, grammarians have struggled to locate this family in English syntax. \textcite[236, 944, 1361 n.\ 70]{huddleston2002} distinguish restricted verbal optatives from \mention{Damn these mosquitoes!}, tentatively treating the latter as an exceptional interjection phrase and doubting an understood subject; \textcite[852--853]{quirk1985} list \mention{Damn (it)}, \mention{Bugger (it)}, and \mention{Fuck (it)} among expletive formulae alongside interjections; formal semanticists model expressive and use-conditional meaning \citep{potts2007,gutzmann2015,gutzmann2019} or the social-relation semantics of profane quasi-verbs while setting morphosyntax aside \citep[4]{portner2026}; and corpus studies treat them as taboo verbs, euphemisms, or interactives \citep{mcenery2006,allanburridge2006,napolihoeksema2009,ljung2011,heine2023interactives}.

Almost every grammatical discussion relies on introspection over \mention{damn} and \mention{fuck}: focusing on \mention{damn}/\mention{bless} invites \mention{God}-ellipsis, focusing on \mention{fuck}/\mention{screw} invites defective verbs, and focusing on \mention{damn it} invites interjections. None has been tested across all thirteen core heads (Section~\ref{sec:diagnostics}), six \mention{fuck}-euphemisms (\mention{frak}, \mention{feck}, \mention{eff}, \mention{frig}, \mention{fudge}, \mention{frick}), archaic \mention{confound}, and non-verbal \mention{yay}/\mention{boo}.

Previous debates also compress four questions into a \enquote{defective verb} vs.\ \enquote{construction} binary: (1) separation from primary interjections, pronoun-restricted expressives, imperatives, and reduced performatives; (2) which extensions (\mention{God}, \mention{to hell}, \mention{for V-ing}, coordination) generalize vs.\ stay lexically anchored; (3) complement-NP openness vs.\ head-slot recruitment and partial euphemistic correspondence; and (4) how live analyses accommodate these uneven distributions.

This comparative corpus study addresses those questions across 19.95M deduplicated words of locally audited corpora (CABNC, BNC Baby XML, OANC), 11.83M words of contemporary unscripted British conversation (Spoken BNC2014), and a 2.225B-word subtitle and web search space (TV Corpus, GloWbE; plus exploratory COCA checks). Its goal is descriptive grammar with explicit uncertainty bounds: mapping what's attested, what's lexically concentrated, and where observational data do and don't separate competing representations.

\subsection{Morphosyntactic diagnostics}
\label{sec:diagnostics}

The thirteen core heads represent three historical strata: theological optatives (\mention{damn}, \mention{bless}, \mention{curse}, \mention{blast}), secular taboo or violent verbs (\mention{fuck}, \mention{screw}, \mention{sod}, \mention{bugger}, \mention{hang}), and minced oaths (\mention{drat}, \mention{darn}, \mention{dash}, \mention{dang}).\footnote{Historically, \mention{drat} contracts \mention{(Go)d rot}, \mention{darn} and \mention{dang} are phonological euphemisms for \mention{damn}, and \mention{dash} entered early nineteenth-century British English as a mild euphemism associated with the printed dash replacing \mention{damn} \citep{mcenery2006,allanburridge2006,ljung2011}.} Across these strata, the subjectless NP pattern displays seven grammatical properties, summarized in (\ref{ex:diagnostics}), where asterisks mark ungrammaticality \emph{on the subjectless imprecative reading}.

\ea\label{ex:diagnostics}
\ea Obligatory accusative pronoun form on case-marking pronouns (descriptive, compatible with default case): Damn him! / Fuck them! / \ungram{Damn he!} / \ungram{Fuck they!}
\ex Open lexical NP complementation: Screw the \emph{Times}! / Sod the bar codes! / Oh, darn the luck! / Confound that man!
\ex Non-reflexive second- and first-person objects (unlike imperatives and speaker-subject reduced performatives): Fuck you! / Damn you all! (vs.\ imperative \mention{Fuck yourself!}); Fuck me! / Bugger me! (vs.\ \ungram{I fuck me!}, predicting \mention{myself})
\ex Absence of tense, agreement, aspect, imperative \mention{do}-negation, and \mention{please} (on the imprecative reading): \ungram{Damned the bell!} / \ungram{Don't damn him!} (imprec.) / \#Please sod the choir!
\ex Absence of ordinary overt subjects (on the imprecative reading): \ungram{I sod the choir!} / \ungram{You fuck you!} (vs.\ imperative \mention{You fuck off!})
\ex No eventive passive preserving imprecative force (\ungram{The rules are being fucked by me!}, imprec.), alongside productive bare-subjunctive passive dismissals (\mention{The law be damned!}, \mention{Sony be buggered!})
\ex Split modification: rejection of event-level manner/time adverbials on \mention{fuck}/\mention{damn} (\ungram{Fuck him quickly!}, \ungram{Damn those irregular verbs tomorrow!}; \citealp[5--6]{quang1971}) alongside evaluative \mention{for V-ing} (\mention{Fuck her for killing herself!}), dismissal-scope temporal adjuncts (\mention{Screw the Times \dots\ for the moment}), and lexically restricted \mention{to hell} (\mention{Damn him to hell!}).
\z
\z

Three points sharpen (\ref{ex:diagnostics}). First, accusative form in (\ref{ex:diagnostics}a) doesn't alone prove verbal Case assignment: English assigns \term{default accusative case} outside nominative positions, including non-verbal expressive small clauses (\mention{Poor me!}, \mention{Silly me!}; \citealp{schutze2001,pottsroeper2006}) and non-verbal \mention{yay me!} / \mention{boo him!} (Section~\ref{sec:dissoc}). What separates (\ref{ex:diagnostics}) both from primary interjections (\mention{ouch}, \mention{oops}, \mention{ugh}, \mention{wow}, \mention{whoops}, \mention{yuck}; \citealp{ameka1992,amekawilkins2006,meinard2015}) and from pronoun-restricted \mention{yay}/\mention{boo} is the combination of obligatory accusative pronominal targets, open determined/possessive lexical NPs, and evaluative \mention{for V-ing}.

Second, non-reflexive objects in (\ref{ex:diagnostics}c) yield two independent subject-omission diagnostics. Non-reflexive second-person \mention{you} separates subjectless imprecatives from ordinary imperatives \citep[3]{quang1971,pinker2007,portner2026}, whose implicit addressee subject binds a reflexive (\mention{Fuck yourself!}). Parallel reasoning applies to non-reflexive first-person \mention{me} (\mention{Fuck me!}, \mention{Bugger me!}, attested in both Tier~1 and \texttt{T3}): a speaker-subject reduced performative (\mention{Thank you} $<$ \mention{I thank you}) predicts reflexive \mention{myself} when the speaker is the target, ruling out speaker-subject omission for secular heads. Literal verbs remain grammatical in (\ref{ex:diagnostics}d--f) on non-imprecative readings (e.g.\ liturgical \mention{Don't damn him!}), while \mention{\#Please sod the choir!} reflects register clash rather than pure syntactic ill-formedness.

Third, (\ref{ex:diagnostics}f--g) qualify two classical claims. Bare-subjunctive passive dismissals (\mention{The law be damned!}, \mention{Sony be buggered!}) confirm that some heads retain passive-participle syntax outside the active subjectless frame (Section~\ref{sec:minced}). And while \textcite[5--6]{quang1971} cited the rejection of event-level manner/time adverbials (\ungram{Damn those irregular verbs tomorrow!}) against verbhood, Sections~\ref{sec:tier1} and \ref{sec:dissoc} document productive evaluative-scope modifiers~-- especially \mention{for V-ing} and frame-level \mention{for the moment}.

\subsection{Live analytical accounts}
\label{sec:four-positions}

Four analytical perspectives frame the literature:

\paragraph{Position A: Optative and reduced-performative subject omission.}
Historically, \mention{bless} and \mention{damn} derive from third-person \mention{God}-optatives \citep{huddleston2002,ljung2011,grosz2012}, while reduced performatives (\mention{Thank you} $<$ \mention{I thank you}) explain non-reflexive \mention{you} and \mention{for V-ing} on verbs of judging \citep{fillmore1971}. Formula-conditioned optative omission remains viable locally on \mention{bless} and \mention{damn}, and \mention{thank you for V-ing} supplies a comparative anchor for reason modification (Section~\ref{sec:dissoc}), whereas non-reflexive \mention{me} rules out speaker-subject omission on secular heads.

\paragraph{Position B: Primary interjections versus complement-retaining interjection phrases.}
Interjection theories separate \term{primary interjections} (\mention{ouch}, \mention{oops}, \mention{ugh}), which don't govern NP complements, from \term{secondary interjections} (\mention{damn!}, \mention{fuck!}, \mention{bless!}) recruited from lexical words \citep{ameka1992,amekawilkins2006,meinard2015,heine2023interactives}. Open lexical NPs exceed unanalysed frozen chunks (\mention{damn it}, \mention{dash it}), while a \emph{complement-retaining} interjection phrase \citep[1361 n.\ 70]{huddleston2002,amekawilkins2006} remains a live rival.

\paragraph{Position C: Modernized lexicalist defective-entry grammar.}
From \textcite[7]{quang1971} (\texttt{Epithet $\to$ Quasi-verb NP}) to modern lexicalist frameworks \citep{mullerwechsler2014}, each living head carries a defective lexical entry selecting an NP in a subjectless frame. \textbf{Missing finite forms don't refute defectiveness}: paradigm gaps define defective verbs (compare \mention{beware}, \mention{betide}), and a developed lexicalist entry can incorporate partial verbal paradigms, horizontal euphemistic links, default accusative case \citep{schutze2001}, and evaluative \mention{for V-ing} \citep{fillmore1971}.

\paragraph{Position D: Usage-based constructional network.}
Modern usage-based Construction Grammar \citep{fillmore1988mechanisms,goldberg1995constructions,goldberg2003,croft2001,bybee2010,diessel2019,goldberg2019,schmid2020} posits a \emph{structured network} pairing schematic \mention{[X\textsubscript{expr} NP]} with lexically anchored sub-constructions (civic \mention{God bless NP}, \mention{damn NP to hell}, British \mention{damn and blast}) and horizontal euphemistic links~-- converging closely with enriched lexicalism (cf.\ Sign-Based Construction Grammar, \citealp{sag2012sign}).

\section{Data and methods}
\label{sec:methods}
% ===========================================================================

No single corpus combines exhaustive, line-level manual verification over POS-tagged and spoken transcripts with the multi-billion-word scale required to observe low-frequency frames such as minced oaths with lexical NPs, lexical-NP \mention{for V-ing}, or \mention{fuck}-euphemisms. I pair two complementary tiers of corpus data.

\subsection{Tier 1: Locally audited British and American corpora (19.95M words)}
\label{sec:tier1-methods}

Tier~1 runs exhaustive extraction under stated query rules across three local corpora: \textbf{CABNC} \citep{albert2015cabnc} (1,860 TalkBank CHAT files from spoken demographic BNC1994; 2,054,817 tokens), \textbf{BNC Baby XML} \citep{burnard2007bncbaby} (182 CLAWS5-tagged files across spoken, fiction, news, and academic prose; 4,001,787 tokens), and \textbf{OANC} \citep{ide2008oanc} (8,824 files of American spoken and written English; 14,449,791 tokens). Removing 12 overlapping BNC Baby \texttt{dem} files (559,070 tokens; listed in the archive) leaves 10,854 disjoint files and \textbf{19,947,325 deduplicated tokens} (5.50M UK; 14.45M US). Inspecting 200 raw CABNC files showed 0/200 contain timed Jeffersonian pauses; BNC1994 \texttt{<s>} unit-initial capitals (\mention{Ouch My foot}) are reported descriptively only because clause boundaries enter coding. CABNC and OANC target closed-class NP starters (\mention{it}, pronouns, determinatives, genitive pronouns in determiner function, quantifiers), while BNC Baby's CLAWS5 tags admit \emph{any} NP-starting tag (including \texttt{NP0} and bare nouns) to calibrate missed heads. All 596 target candidates and 148 primary-interjection candidates (\mention{ouch}, \mention{oops}, \mention{ugh}, \mention{wow}, \mention{whoops}, \mention{yuck}) were manually adjudicated at line level, with Python verifying arithmetic ($\text{raw} = \text{verified} + \text{God} + \text{excluded}$).

\subsection{Tier 2: Subtitle, web, and contemporary spoken corpora}
\label{sec:tier2-methods}

Tier~2 pairs a 2.225-billion-word subtitle and web search space (plus exploratory COCA checks; \citealp{davies2020coca}) with an 11.83-million-word unscripted contemporary British benchmark (exact queries in Table~\ref{tab:appendix-queries} and the Appendix):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The TV Corpus} \citep{davies2019tv}: 325M words of six-variety television \emph{subtitles} (1950--2018). Thirty-one queries cover baselines (\texttt{T1a}--\texttt{T8b}), follow-ups (\texttt{F1a}--\texttt{F5c}), and controls (\texttt{C1a}--\texttt{C3b}, \texttt{D1}--\texttt{D4}). Because default KWIC displays cap at 100 nonrandom lines (and fewer on some queries, e.g.\ 85/104 in \texttt{T7a}, 72/99 in \texttt{T7b}), audits pair inspected lines with deterministic undisplayed-hit bounds.
\item \textbf{GloWbE} \citep{davies2013glowbe,daviesfuchs2015}: 1.9B words of web English across 20 varieties. Six queries (\texttt{G1}--\texttt{G4}, \texttt{C4}) probe minced oaths (\mention{drat}, \mention{darn}, \mention{dash}, \mention{dang}); because country tables lacked concordances under access limits, \texttt{G1}/\texttt{G2} serve strictly as lexically screened \term{candidate attestations}.
\item \textbf{Spoken BNC2014} \citep{loveEtAl2017SpokenBNC2014}: 11.83M tokens of unscripted British conversation (2012--2016), queried via Sketch Engine (\texttt{preloaded/bnc2014\_spoken\_tt3}) using CQL (\texttt{B2-1a}--\texttt{B2-5b}) to calibrate TV period-drama and broadcast-censorship effects against living spontaneous speech.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Coding taxonomy, exclusions, and quantitative reporting}
\label{sec:coding}

Verified Tier-1 subjectless tokens are coded into seven mutually exclusive target categories (combining formal structure with two phraseological priority classes~-- routine \mention{bless you} over generic \mention{you}, and possessive body-part/soul formulae over residual full NPs): \mention{it}-targets (fused \mention{damn it}, \mention{fuck it}, \mention{sod it}, plus referential \mention{And bless it}); routine \mention{bless you} (sneeze, parting, gratitude); second-person \mention{you} (\mention{fuck you}, \mention{damn you}); case-marking accusative pronouns (\mention{fuck me}, \mention{bugger him}, \mention{bless her}); fused determiner-head demonstratives (\mention{sod this}, \mention{bugger that}); possessive body-part/soul formulae (\mention{bless his heart}, \mention{bless his cotton socks}); and remaining open full lexical NPs (\mention{Damn the bell!}, \mention{Screw the Cabinet}). Overt \mention{God + HEAD + NP} optatives are tallied separately.

Exclusions remove literal/finite transitive verbs (\mention{to hang the curtains}, \mention{to darn her own stockings}); negative quantifier idioms (\mention{bugger/sod/fuck all}); standalone interjections before a new clause (\mention{Fuck. That would have been amusing}); metalinguistic citations (\emph{Verbatim}); proper-noun homonyms (UK retailer \emph{Dash}); and West African ditransitive \mention{dash} (\enquote{give/tip}) in GloWbE. Because tokens cluster within files, formulae, franchises, and series, and Tier-2 concordances reflect nonrandom default display and exploratory follow-ups, I don't report independent-token significance tests (\emph{p}-values). Instead, I report exact counts, proportions, explicit denominators, document/series dispersion, and~-- for the primary PP contrast~-- deterministic bounds over all undisplayed hits, distinguishing the 2.225-billion-word search space from each comparison's audited sample size. Zero cells are read as nonattestations under the stated queries unless an independent baseline establishes expected counts.

\subsection{Grammatical boundaries and head-slot recruitment}
\label{sec:ruled-out}

Table~\ref{tab:verdict} establishes clear boundaries alongside explicit underdetermination among surviving accounts. Non-reflexive second-person \mention{you} rules out imperatives, non-reflexive first-person \mention{me} (\mention{Fuck me!}, \mention{Bugger me!}) rules out speaker-subject reduced performatives on secular heads, and open lexical-NP complementation (24 Tier-1 full-NP tokens plus audited Tier-2 concordances) separates the paradigm from primary interjections (0/148 local; 0/53 TV \mention{him/her/them} candidates) and pronoun-restricted \mention{yay}/\mention{boo} (0/13 lexical-NP hits in \texttt{D3}). The head slot expands via (i) \textbf{verbal recruitment}~-- both taboo/expressive \citep{mcenery2006,napolihoeksema2009,ljung2011} and archaic non-taboo (\mention{confound}, \texttt{D4})~-- and (ii) \textbf{euphemistic correspondence} across \mention{damn} and \mention{fuck}. Beyond those boundaries, three live analyses remain compatible with the corpus asymmetries: a \emph{complement-retaining secondary-interjection phrase} \citep[1361 n.\ 70]{huddleston2002,amekawilkins2006}, a \emph{lexicalist defective-entry grammar} (Position~C), and a \emph{structured constructional network} (Position~D), alongside local formula-conditioned optatives on \mention{bless}/\mention{damn} and the \mention{thank you} anchor (\texttt{C2a}) for reason modification.

\subsection{Convergence of lexicalist and constructionist accounts}
\label{sec:convergence}

The central theoretical result is that \textbf{observational corpus data underdetermine the choice between Position~C (modernized lexicalist defective entries) and structured Position~D (a usage-based constructional network)}~-- much as sign-based frameworks place lexical entries and constructions on a continuum \citep{sag2012sign}. Under \textbf{Position~C} \citep{quang1971,mullerwechsler2014}, each head carries a defective entry (\texttt{Epithet $\to$ Quasi-verb NP}): missing finite forms follow from defectiveness; formulaic \mention{be darned} (\texttt{F5a}) and marginal \mention{Rules be darned} (\texttt{D2a}) fit retained participial cells; pronouns receive default accusative case \citep{schutze2001}; \mention{for V-ing} reflects judgment/retaliation-frame modification \citep{fillmore1971}; and lexical asymmetries and euphemistic links reside in the lexicon. Under \textbf{structured Position~D} \citep{goldberg2003,goldberg2019,croft2001,bybee2010,diessel2019,schmid2020}, schematic \mention{[X\textsubscript{expr} NP]} links vertically to anchored sub-constructions (civic \mention{God bless NP}, \mention{damn NP to hell}, \mention{damn and blast}) and horizontally to euphemistic allolexes.

Even experimental coercion of novel non-verbal heads wouldn't automatically separate Position~C from Position~D without fixing auxiliary assumptions, since lexicalist grammars permit productive lexical rules \citep{mullerwechsler2014} while usage-based networks can restrict head slots to entrenched items \citep{goldberg2019}. Testing how speakers extend lexical-NP complementation and \mention{for V-ing}~-- comparing \mention{yay}/\mention{boo} and preposition-headed dismissals (\mention{to hell with X}, \mention{down with X})~-- remains a productive experimental agenda, while the descriptive corpus contribution stands independently.

\section{Limitations and conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ===========================================================================

Four bounds remain: (1) while Spoken BNC2014 calibrates contemporary unscripted British speech against TV period drama and broadcast censorship, a comparable large contemporary American spoken benchmark remains for future work; (2) GloWbE lacked concordances during collection, leaving \texttt{G1}/\texttt{G2} as screened candidates and excluding context-free \mention{dash NP}; (3) a single coder performed adjudication, so multi-coder reliability on a stratified sample would strengthen borderline distinctions; and (4) corpus counts measure attestation rather than graded acceptability, leaving inanimate-target \mention{for V-ing} (\mention{Damn this printer for jamming again!}) and prosodic/attachment diagnostics for targeted judgment and audio work.

Within those bounds, this comparative corpus map establishes the empirical profile of English subjectless imprecatives and benedictives across thirteen core heads, six \mention{fuck}-euphemisms, and archaic \mention{confound}, calibrated across 19.95M local words, 11.83M words of contemporary British conversation, and 2.225B words of subtitles and web text: an open NP slot separating them from primary interjections and \mention{yay}/\mention{boo}; non-reflexive \mention{you} and \mention{me} ruling out imperatives and speaker-subject reduced performatives; a bounded 69.5-percentage-point head-composition split between \mention{damn}-concentrated \mention{to hell} and six-head evaluative \mention{for V-ing}; expression-specific concentration of overt \mention{God}; and partial euphemistic correspondence conditioned by register and censorship.
