\begin{abstract}
English subjectless imprecatives and benedictives~-- expressions without an overt subject in which an expressive head combines directly with an NP, such as \mention{Damn the bell!}, \mention{Fuck the doctors!}, \mention{Screw you for putting me in this position!}, \mention{Sod the choir!}, \mention{Bless his little cotton socks!}, and euphemistic \mention{Oh, darn the luck!} or \mention{Frak the Gods!}~-- combine obligatory accusative pronouns and open lexical NPs with defective verbal syntax. Previous accounts rely mostly on introspection over \mention{damn} and \mention{fuck}. This article maps thirteen core heads plus six \mention{fuck}-euphemisms across 19.95~million deduplicated words of locally audited British and American corpora (CABNC, BNC Baby XML, OANC) and 2.225~billion words of television subtitles and web English (TV Corpus, GloWbE), plus an exploratory COCA check. Five findings organize the paradigm. First, these heads separate cleanly from primary interjections (0/148 local and 0/53 audited TV direct-NP complements), take non-reflexive second-person \mention{you} (\mention{Fuck you!} vs.\ imperative \mention{Fuck yourself!}), and stand alongside bare-subjunctive passive dismissals (\mention{The law be damned!}). Second, qualifying \textcite[5--6]{quang1971}, they reject event-level manner/time adverbials while hosting evaluative \mention{for V-ing} reason PPs across six heads~-- including secular \mention{fuck} and \mention{screw}, whose ordinary verbal valences lack a judgment reason \citep{fillmore1971}~-- alongside praise/blame formulae (\mention{shame on}, \mention{good for/on}, \mention{kudos to}, \mention{silly me}), contrasting with \mention{damn}-concentrated \mention{to hell} on matched pronouns. Third, overt \mention{God} is virtually absent from secular heads and splits sharply across eight illustrative \mention{bless} expressions. Fourth, euphemistic mirroring replicates across both \mention{damn}- and \mention{fuck}-variants. Finally, these asymmetries eliminate unconditioned \mention{God}-ellipsis, primary-interjection accounts, and a lexically insensitive baseline, while constraining both lexicalist defective-entry grammars and structured constructional networks.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} imprecatives, benedictives, construction grammar, defective verbs, English syntax

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ===========================================================================

English contains a pragmatically vivid family of expressions without an overt grammatical subject in which an expressive head combines directly with a noun phrase (which may itself be determined, possessive, or pronominal) and, optionally, a prepositional phrase. Attested examples span secular taboo maledictives, theological curses, affectionate benedictives, and mild minced oaths, as illustrated in (\ref{ex:intro-paradigm}).

\ea\label{ex:intro-paradigm}
\ea Damn the bell! \hfill (BNC Baby \texttt{H85})
\ex Fuck the doctors. \hfill (BNC Baby \texttt{CCW})
\ex Sod the choir! \hfill (BNC Baby \texttt{KPU})
\ex Bugger the rest! \hfill (CABNC \texttt{KB7})
\ex Screw the Cabinet and the rest. \hfill (OANC \texttt{Slate})
\ex Bless you for helping me out with the Watsons. \hfill (BNC Baby \texttt{G0Y})
\ex Darn you for raising us so well. \hfill (TV Corpus, \emph{Good Luck Charlie})
\z
\z

Since \textcite[3--7]{quang1971} analysed \mention{fuck you} and \mention{damn him} as \enquote{English sentences without overt grammatical subject} headed by a special category of \enquote{quasi-verbs}, grammarians have struggled to locate this family in English syntax. Standard reference grammars and semantic studies diverge sharply. \textcite[236, 944, 1361 n.\ 70]{huddleston2002} distinguish restricted verbal optatives from \mention{Damn these mosquitoes!}, tentatively treating the latter as an exceptional interjection phrase and doubting the motivation for an understood subject; \textcite[852--853]{quirk1985} list \mention{Damn (it)}, \mention{Bugger (it)}, and \mention{Fuck (it)} among expletive formulae alongside interjections; formal semanticists model expressive and use-conditional meaning \citep{potts2007,gutzmann2015,gutzmann2019} or the dynamic social-relation semantics of profane quasi-verbs while setting morphosyntax aside \citep[4]{portner2026}; and corpus and grammaticalization studies treat them as taboo verbs, euphemisms, or interactives \citep{mcenery2006,allanburridge2006,napolihoeksema2009,ljung2011,heine2023interactives}.

Almost every grammatical discussion has relied on introspection over two high-frequency heads~-- \mention{damn} and \mention{fuck}~-- without mapping the wider paradigm in attested English. Concentrating on \mention{damn} or \mention{bless} invites a \mention{God}-ellipsis account; concentrating on \mention{fuck} or \mention{screw} invites a defective-verb account; concentrating on frozen \mention{damn it} invites an interjection account. None of those extrapolations has been tested systematically across the thirteen-head paradigm~-- theological \mention{damn}, \mention{bless}, \mention{curse}, \mention{blast}; secular taboo or violent \mention{fuck}, \mention{screw}, \mention{sod}, \mention{bugger}, \mention{hang}; and minced \mention{drat}, \mention{darn}, \mention{dash}, \mention{dang}~-- nor across parallel \mention{fuck}-euphemisms (\mention{frak}, \mention{feck}, \mention{eff}, \mention{frig}, \mention{fudge}, \mention{frick}).

Previous debates also compress four distinct questions into a binary choice between \enquote{defective verb} and \enquote{construction}: (1) how the family separates from primary interjections, imperatives, and frozen formulae; (2) which extensions (\mention{God}-subjects, \mention{to hell}, \mention{for V-ing}, coordination) generalize across heads versus stay lexically anchored; (3) how complement-NP openness differs from head-slot recruitment across taboo families; and (4) which theoretical baselines the asymmetries eliminate, and where lexicalist and constructionist accounts converge.

This article answers those questions across 19.95~million deduplicated words of locally audited British and American corpora (CABNC, BNC Baby XML, OANC) paired with 2.225~billion words of television subtitles and web English (TV Corpus, GloWbE; 2.245~billion words total, plus an exploratory COCA check). The corpus facts eliminate unconditioned \mention{God}-ellipsis, primary-interjection accounts, and a lexically insensitive baseline, qualify \textcite[5--6]{quang1971} by linking \mention{for V-ing} to judgment-predication semantics \citep{fillmore1971}, replicate euphemistic mirroring across both \mention{damn} and \mention{fuck}, and map the constraints shared by lexicalist and constructionist grammars.

\subsection{Morphosyntactic diagnostics}
\label{sec:diagnostics}

The thirteen core heads represent three historical strata: theological optatives (\mention{damn}, \mention{bless}, \mention{curse}, \mention{blast}), secular taboo or violent verbs (\mention{fuck}, \mention{screw}, \mention{sod}, \mention{bugger}, \mention{hang}), and minced oaths (\mention{drat}, \mention{darn}, \mention{dash}, \mention{dang}).\footnote{Historically, \mention{drat} contracts \mention{(Go)d rot}, \mention{darn} and \mention{dang} are phonological euphemisms for \mention{damn}, and \mention{dash} entered early nineteenth-century British English as a mild euphemism associated with the printed dash replacing \mention{damn} \citep{mcenery2006,allanburridge2006,ljung2011}.} Across these strata, the subjectless NP pattern displays seven grammatical properties, summarized in (\ref{ex:diagnostics}), where asterisks mark ungrammaticality \emph{on the subjectless imprecative reading}.

\ea\label{ex:diagnostics}
\ea Obligatory accusative pronoun form (on case-marking pronouns): Damn him! / Fuck them! / \ungram{Damn he!} / \ungram{Fuck they!}
\ex Open lexical NP complementation: Screw the \emph{Times}! / Sod the bar codes! / Oh, darn the luck! / Frak the Gods!
\ex Non-reflexive second-person object (unlike imperatives): Fuck you! / Damn you all! vs.\ imperative Fuck yourself! (\ungram{Fuck you!} as an ordinary reflexive imperative)
\ex Absence of tense, agreement, aspect, imperative \mention{do}-negation, and \mention{please} (on the imprecative reading): \ungram{Damned the bell!} / \ungram{Don't damn him!} (imprec.) / \#Please sod the choir!
\ex Absence of ordinary overt subjects (on the imprecative reading): \ungram{I sod the choir!} / \ungram{You fuck you!} (vs.\ imperative \mention{You fuck off!})
\ex No eventive passive preserving imprecative force (\ungram{The rules are being fucked by me!}, imprec.), alongside productive bare-subjunctive passive dismissals (\mention{The law be damned!}, \mention{Sony be buggered!})
\ex Split modification: rejection of event-level manner/time adverbials on \mention{fuck}/\mention{damn} (\ungram{Fuck him quickly!}, \ungram{Damn those irregular verbs tomorrow!}; \citealp[5--6]{quang1971}) alongside evaluative \mention{for V-ing} (\mention{Fuck her for killing herself!}), dismissal-scope temporal adjuncts (\mention{Screw the Times \dots\ for the moment}), and lexically restricted \mention{to hell} (\mention{Damn him to hell!}).
\z
\z

Three points sharpen (\ref{ex:diagnostics}). First, accusative form in (\ref{ex:diagnostics}a) doesn't by itself prove transitive verbal Case assignment. English assigns \term{default accusative case} outside nominative subject positions, including non-verbal expressive small clauses such as \mention{Poor me!} and \mention{Silly me!} \citep{schutze2001,pottsroeper2006}. What separates (\ref{ex:diagnostics}) from primary interjections like \mention{ouch}, \mention{oops}, \mention{ugh}, \mention{wow}, \mention{whoops}, and \mention{yuck} \citep{ameka1992,amekawilkins2006,meinard2015} is the bundle of obligatory accusative pronominal targets (\ungram{Ouch him!}, \ungram{Ugh them!}), open determined/possessive lexical NPs integrated with the head, and evaluative \mention{for V-ing}.

Second, non-reflexive second-person \mention{you} in (\ref{ex:diagnostics}c) cleanly distinguishes subjectless imprecatives from ordinary imperatives \citep[3]{quang1971,pinker2007,portner2026}. Imperatives carry an implicit addressee subject that binds a co-referential object into a reflexive (\mention{Fuck yourself!}), whereas subjectless imprecatives take non-reflexive \mention{you} (which is number-neutral: \mention{Damn you all!}, \mention{Eff you both!}). Note that ordinary literal verbs remain grammatical in (\ref{ex:diagnostics}d--f) on non-imprecative readings (e.g.\ liturgical \mention{Don't damn him!} or declarative \mention{You screw the rules}), while \mention{\#Please sod the choir!} reflects register and illocutionary clash rather than pure syntactic ill-formedness (compare \mention{Please fuck off!}).

Third, (\ref{ex:diagnostics}f--g) clarify two classical claims. Bare-subjunctive passive dismissals (\mention{The law be damned!}, \mention{Arteries be damned!}, \mention{Sony be buggered!}) confirm that at least some heads retain passive-participle syntax outside the active subjectless frame (Section~\ref{sec:minced}). And while \textcite[5--6]{quang1971} argued against verbhood on the grounds that \mention{fuck} and \mention{damn} reject event-level manner, location, and time adverbials (\ungram{Damn those irregular verbs tomorrow!}), Sections~\ref{sec:tier1} and \ref{sec:dissoc} show that modifiers scoping over the \emph{evaluative act} or dismissal~-- especially \mention{for V-ing} reason PPs and frame-level \mention{for the moment}~-- are attested productively across multiple heads.

\subsection{Four analytical positions and baselines}
\label{sec:four-positions}

To map how the corpus facts constrain grammatical theory without attacking strawmen, I compare two theoretical baselines (Positions~A and~B) against two developed grammatical architectures (Positions~C and~D):

\paragraph{Position A: Unconditioned \mention{God}-optative ellipsis (baseline).}
Historically, \mention{bless} and \mention{damn} derive from third-person optative clauses with subject \mention{God} \citep{huddleston2002,ljung2011,grosz2012}. As a synchronic baseline across the paradigm, unconditioned \mention{God}-ellipsis predicts either broad overt \mention{God}-alternation across active heads or unconditioned variation between overt \mention{God} and bare forms on theological heads. (A more constrained optative account allowing formula- and register-conditioned subject omission survives locally on \mention{bless} and \mention{damn}, as discussed in Section~\ref{sec:god-split}.)

\paragraph{Position B: Primary interjections and secondary-interjection analyses.}
Interjection theories separate \term{primary interjections} (\mention{ouch}, \mention{oops}, \mention{ugh}), which lack lexical homonyms and don't govern NP complements, from \term{secondary interjections} (\mention{damn!}, \mention{fuck!}, \mention{bless!}), which recruit lexical words into expressive utterances \citep{ameka1992,amekawilkins2006,meinard2015,heine2023interactives}. Two sub-positions must be kept apart: a \emph{frozen-formula} secondary-interjection view (treating \mention{damn it}, \mention{dash it}, and \mention{bless you} as unanalysed chunks) versus a \emph{complement-retaining} secondary-interjection view (such as \textcite[1361 n.\ 70]{huddleston2002}'s tentative exceptional interjection phrase, or cross-linguistic argument-taking interjections noted by \citealp{amekawilkins2006}). Open lexical NPs rule out primary interjections and frozen formulae, while leaving complement-retaining interjection phrases as a live descriptive option.

\paragraph{Position C: Modernized lexicalist defective-entry grammar.}
In the lexicalist tradition from \textcite[7]{quang1971} (\texttt{Epithet $\to$ Quasi-verb NP}) to lexicalist argument-structure frameworks \citep{mullerwechsler2014}, each living head carries a sense-specific defective lexical entry selecting an NP target in a subjectless frame. \textbf{Showing that a head lacks ordinary finite or past-tense forms doesn't refute a defective-verb analysis}: paradigm gaps are the defining property of defectiveness (compare English \mention{beware} and \mention{betide}). A developed lexicalist account can incorporate (i) partial verbal paradigms (including passive/formulaic \mention{-ed} cells), (ii) horizontal euphemistic lexical links (\mention{damn} $\leftrightarrow$ \mention{darn/dang}, \mention{fuck} $\leftrightarrow$ \mention{frak/feck/eff/frig/fudge/frick}), (iii) English default accusative case \citep{schutze2001}, and (iv) judgment-frame or evaluative \mention{for V-ing} modifiers \citep{fillmore1971}.

\paragraph{Position D: Usage-based constructional network (vs.\ lexically insensitive baseline).}
Under Construction Grammar \citep{fillmore1988mechanisms,goldberg1995constructions,goldberg2003,croft2001}, the pairing of an expressive head and its NP target forms a stored constructional pattern. As a deliberately simple stipulated baseline, a \emph{lexically insensitive schema} would predict uniform productivity across all active heads for \mention{God}-subjects, \mention{to hell}, \mention{for V-ing}, and coordination. Modern usage-based construction grammar \citep{bybee2010,diessel2019,goldberg2019,schmid2020} instead posits a \emph{structured network} combining a schematic subjectless expressive pattern \mention{[X\textsubscript{expr} NP]}, lexically anchored sub-constructions (civic \mention{God bless NP}, \mention{damn NP to hell}, British \mention{damn and blast}), and horizontal euphemistic links~-- a representation that converges closely with an enriched lexicalist network (cf.\ Sign-Based Construction Grammar, \citealp{sag2012sign}). Section~\ref{sec:discussion} evaluates all four positions against the corpus diagnostics in Table~\ref{tab:verdict}.

\section{Data and methods}
\label{sec:methods}
% ===========================================================================

No single corpus combines exhaustive, line-level manual verification over POS-tagged and spoken transcripts with the multi-billion-word scale required to observe low-frequency frames such as minced oaths with lexical NPs, lexical-NP \mention{for V-ing}, or \mention{fuck}-euphemisms. I pair two complementary tiers of corpus data.

\subsection{Tier 1: Locally audited British and American corpora (19.95M words)}
\label{sec:tier1-methods}

Tier~1 executes exhaustive extraction under stated query rules across three local corpora:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{CABNC} \citep{albert2015cabnc}: 1,860 TalkBank CHAT files of spoken demographic British English from BNC1994 (246,783 utterances; 2,054,817 word tokens).
\item \textbf{BNC Baby XML} \citep{burnard2007bncbaby}: 182 CLAWS5 POS-tagged XML files spanning demographic spoken (\texttt{dem}), fiction (\texttt{fic}), newspaper (\texttt{news}), and academic prose (\texttt{aca}), totalling 4,001,787 tokens.
\item \textbf{OANC} \citep{ide2008oanc}: 8,824 plain-text files of American spoken (Switchboard, Charlotte) and written English (\emph{Slate}, \emph{Verbatim}, fiction, non-fiction, technical prose), totalling 14,449,791 word tokens.
\end{itemize}

Three safeguards govern Tier~1. First, 12 of BNC Baby's 30 \texttt{dem} files (\texttt{KB5}, \texttt{KB7}, \texttt{KBC}, \texttt{KBD}, \texttt{KBH}, \texttt{KBJ}, \texttt{KBP}, \texttt{KBW}, \texttt{KCC}, \texttt{KCF}, \texttt{KE2}, \texttt{KE4}; 559,070 tokens) overlap CABNC and are removed from BNC Baby, yielding 10,854 disjoint files and \textbf{19,947,325 deduplicated word tokens} (5.50~million UK; 14.45~million US). Second, inspecting 200 raw CABNC \texttt{.cha} files showed 0/200 contain timed Jeffersonian pauses; they preserve BNC1994 \texttt{<s>} unit-initial capitalization (\mention{Ouch My foot}), which I report descriptively only because clause boundaries enter coding. Third, CABNC and OANC queries target closed-class NP starters (\mention{it}, pronouns, demonstratives, articles, possessives, quantifiers), whereas BNC Baby's CLAWS5 tags accept \emph{any} NP-starting tag~-- including \texttt{NP0} and bare nouns~-- to calibrate what closed-class filters miss. All 596 raw target candidates and 148 primary-interjection candidates (\mention{ouch}, \mention{oops}, \mention{ugh}, \mention{wow}, \mention{whoops}, \mention{yuck}) were manually adjudicated at line level, with Python enforcing bookkeeping ($\text{raw} = \text{verified} + \text{God} + \text{excluded}$).

\subsection{Tier 2: Large-scale subtitle and web corpora (2.225B words)}
\label{sec:tier2-methods}

Tier~2 scales the investigation across 2.225~billion words in two Mark Davies corpora, supplemented by a small exploratory COCA check \citep{davies2020coca} (every exact query string appears in Table~\ref{tab:appendix-queries}):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The TV Corpus} \citep{davies2019tv}: 325~million words of British, Irish, American, Canadian, Australian, and New Zealand television \emph{subtitles} (1950--2018). Eleven baseline queries (\texttt{T1a}--\texttt{T8b}) and nine follow-up queries (\texttt{F1a}--\texttt{F5c}) cover pronouns, lexical NPs, overt \mention{God}, \mention{to hell} vs.\ \mention{for V-ing}, non-imprecative evaluative controls, six \mention{fuck}-euphemisms, participial cells, a finite baseline, and \mention{dash it}. Because the interface displays up to 100 KWIC lines in default order rather than a random sample, KWIC precision characterizes the inspected lines rather than all undisplayed hits.
\item \textbf{GloWbE} \citep{davies2013glowbe,daviesfuchs2015}: 1.9~billion words of web English across 20 national varieties. Five queries (\texttt{G1}--\texttt{G4}) probe minced oaths (\mention{drat}, \mention{darn}, \mention{dash}, \mention{dang}) in lexical-NP, accusative-pronoun, attributive/adverbial mirroring, and past-tense frames. Because the web interface returned country tables without concordances under access limits during collection, \texttt{G1} and \texttt{G2} serve strictly as lexically screened \term{candidate attestations}~-- never verified headline examples~-- while \texttt{G4} pairs with KWIC-audited \texttt{F5b}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Coding taxonomy, exclusions, and statistical protocol}
\label{sec:coding}

Verified Tier-1 subjectless tokens are classified into seven mutually exclusive formal categories: \mention{it}-targets (fused non-referential \mention{damn it}, \mention{fuck it}, \mention{sod it}, plus one referential \mention{And bless it}); routine \mention{bless you} (sneeze, parting, and gratitude, including \mention{Bless you for helping me out with the Watsons}); second-person \mention{you} (\mention{fuck you}, \mention{damn you}); unambiguous case-marking accusative pronouns (\mention{fuck me}, \mention{bugger him}, \mention{bless her}); bare demonstratives (\mention{sod this}, \mention{bugger that}); possessive body-part/soul formulae (\mention{bless his heart}, \mention{bless his cotton socks}, \mention{damn your eyes}); and open full lexical NPs (\mention{Damn the bell!}, \mention{Screw the Cabinet}). Overt \mention{God + HEAD + NP} optatives are tallied separately.

Exclusions remove: literal or finite transitive verbs (\mention{to hang the curtains}, \mention{won't screw it up}, \mention{to darn her own stockings}); negative quantifier idioms (\mention{bugger all}, \mention{sod all}, \mention{fuck all}); standalone interjections followed by a new clause (\mention{Fuck. That would have been amusing}); metalinguistic mentions and dictionary glosses (such as \emph{Verbatim} citations of \mention{Curse it!} or \mention{Drat you and your dog!}); proper-noun homonyms (e.g.\ CABNC \mention{Very pretty, Dash, that}, referring to the UK clothing retailer \emph{Dash}); and West African English ditransitive \mention{dash} (\enquote{give a gift/tip}) in GloWbE. Because corpus tokens cluster within files, fixed formulae, and television series, exact two-sided Fisher's exact tests are reported only on matched-denominator contingency tables alongside document/series dispersion and explicitly interpreted as descriptive strength indicators rather than assuming full token independence.

\section{Tier-1 results: Local baseline, boundaries, and regional structure}
\label{sec:tier1}
% ===========================================================================

\subsection{Separation from primary interjections and CABNC unit boundaries}
\label{sec:cabnc-boundaries}

Across all 19.95~million deduplicated Tier-1 words, the six primary-interjection comparators (\mention{ouch}, \mention{oops}, \mention{ugh}, \mention{wow}, \mention{whoops}, \mention{yuck}) produce 148 raw \mention{PRIMARY + NP-starter} sequences (23 CABNC, 16 deduplicated BNC Baby, 109 OANC). Manual inspection confirms \textbf{0 verified direct-NP complements (0/148)}: every sequence is a finite-clause subject (\mention{Whoops, you've dropped your pencil}; \mention{Ugh, it's slimy}), a nursery formula (\mention{oops/whoops a daisy}), a lexical verb (\mention{to wow us}, \mention{to yuck it up}), or a detached verbless exclamative (\mention{Ouch. \subsection{BNC Baby POS calibration, regional notes, and Tier-1 \mentionhead{God}-optatives}
\label{sec:tier1-regional}

BNC Baby's CLAWS5 scan calibrates what closed-class \mention{PRON/DET} queries miss. Accepting \texttt{NP0} and bare common nouns recovered three genuine tokens~-- \mention{Damn McIllvanney} (\texttt{CCW:483}, \texttt{NP0}), \mention{Oh, screw Jesus Christ} (\texttt{HR9:2136}, \texttt{NP0}), and bedtime prayer \mention{night, bless mummy and dad} (\texttt{KD3:2485}, \texttt{NN1})~-- while exposing an important structural ambiguity: \mention{Damn DTs.} (\texttt{GVL:2716}, \texttt{NP0}) can be parsed either as a subjectless imprecative or as a verbless NP with expressive prenominal \mention{damn} (compare \mention{Damn rubbish.}, \texttt{CFY:593}, \mention{Damn nuisance.}, \texttt{CFY:992}, and \citealp[35 n.\ 29]{portner2026} on \mention{Damn Lyndon Johnson}). Tier~1 also shows strong regional asymmetries, though register differences between casual British BNC1994 demographic talk and American Switchboard/written OANC prevent treating raw counts as a register-controlled UK--US comparison. Within Tier~1, \mention{sod} (20 UK vs.\ 1 US) and \mention{bugger} (20 UK vs.\ 1 US) appear almost exclusively in the British subcorpora (the two OANC tokens both occur in \emph{Slate}), while \mention{bless} splits between British \mention{bless him/her} and \mention{bless his/her (little) cotton socks} versus 22 American Switchboard tokens of \mention{bless your/his/her/their (little) heart(s)}. Taken together, \texttt{F1a}, \texttt{F1b}, and \texttt{F2} refine two conclusions. For the grammatical analysis, the key fact is that secular \mention{fuck} (17 shared-pronoun / 20 total audited tokens) and \mention{screw} (13 / 14) carry \emph{no} judgment-Reason \mention{for V-ing} in their ordinary transitive verbal valences, but adopt it productively inside the subjectless imprecative frame. Meanwhile, \texttt{F2}'s 13 retrieved strings all happen to target animate or metonymic human referents, and because \texttt{F2} conditions on the presence of \mention{for V-ing} without an inanimate-dismissal denominator, it can't establish a categorical animacy ban (constructed challenges such as \mention{Damn this printer for jamming again!} or \mention{Screw those rules for making appeals impossible!} suggest that causal responsibility construal, rather than strict animacy, is the testable hypothesis for future judgment work).

Second, these findings qualify \textcite[5--6]{quang1971} precisely: Quang's examples show that subjectless \mention{fuck} and \mention{damn} resist event-level manner, location, and time adverbials (\ungram{Damn those irregular verbs tomorrow!}), whereas modifiers scoping over the evaluative act (\mention{for V-ing}) or the dismissal frame (\mention{for the moment} in (\ref{ex:tier1-gems}e)) are attested productively. And as \mention{Eff you all to H!} in (\ref{ex:f3-examples}c) below demonstrates, even \mention{to hell}'s strong lexical concentration on \mention{damn} in \texttt{T7a} isn't an airtight syntactic boundary once intervening quantifiers (\mention{you all}) and euphemistic elaboration (\mention{to H!}) are considered.

\subsection{What the corpus asymmetries rule out~-- and leave open}
\label{sec:ruled-out}

Three simple baselines fail against Table~\ref{tab:verdict}: \textbf{unconditioned \mention{God}-ellipsis (Position~A)} fails across heads and within \mention{bless}; a \textbf{lexically insensitive schema} fails on \mention{to hell}, shared-pronoun coordination, overt \mention{God}, and regional micro-constructions; and \textbf{primary-interjection or frozen-formula views (narrow Position~B)} can't cover open lexical NPs or productive \mention{for V-ing} on \mention{fuck} and \mention{screw}. By contrast, a \emph{complement-retaining secondary-interjection} analysis \citep[1361 n.\ 70]{huddleston2002,amekawilkins2006} remains a live descriptive rival alongside Positions~C and~D.

\subsection{Complement openness versus head-slot recruitment}
\label{sec:productivity}

Separating \textbf{complement-NP openness} from \textbf{head-slot productivity} clarifies the paradigm. The \textbf{complement NP slot} admits varied definite, demonstrative, possessive, coordinated, and proper-name NPs across living heads (24 Tier-1 full-NP tokens plus audited Tier-2 concordances). The \textbf{head slot}, by contrast, excludes the six primary interjections across 345~million searched words (0/148 Tier-1; 0/53 audited TV) and expands via (i) \textbf{taboo and expressive verbal recruitment} \citep{mcenery2006,napolihoeksema2009,ljung2011} and (ii) \textbf{euphemistic substitution and lexical-variant mirroring} across both \mention{damn} and \mention{fuck}, copying a source lexeme's wider profile without developing ordinary finite curse inflection.

\subsection{Convergence of lexicalist and constructionist accounts}
\label{sec:convergence}

The central theoretical result is that \textbf{observational corpus data underdetermine the choice between Position~C (a modernized lexicalist defective-entry grammar) and structured Position~D (a usage-based constructional network)}~-- much as sign-based frameworks treat lexical entries and phrasal constructions as points on a continuum \citep{sag2012sign}. Under \textbf{Position~C} \citep{quang1971,mullerwechsler2014}, each living head carries a defective lexical entry restricted to a subjectless expressive clause (\texttt{Epithet $\to$ Quasi-verb NP}): missing finite forms (\texttt{G4}, \texttt{F5b}) follow from defectiveness; formulaic \mention{be darned/danged} (\texttt{F5a}) are compatible with retained participial cells; accusative pronouns receive default case \citep{schutze2001}; \mention{for V-ing} reflects judgment-frame Reason modification \citep{fillmore1971} inherited or extended analogically onto \mention{fuck}/\mention{screw}; and all lexical asymmetries and euphemistic links reside in the lexicon. Under \textbf{structured Position~D} \citep{goldberg2003,goldberg2019,croft2001,bybee2010,diessel2019,schmid2020}, speakers generalize a schematic expressive construction \mention{[X\textsubscript{expr} NP]} whose judgment-predication semantics directly explains why non-judgment verbs like \mention{fuck} and \mention{screw} host \mention{for V-ing} inside this frame, linked vertically to lexically anchored sub-constructions (civic \mention{God bless NP}, \mention{damn NP to hell}, British \mention{damn and blast}) and horizontally to euphemistic allolexes.

Even experimental coercion of novel non-verbal heads wouldn't automatically settle Position~C against Position~D without fixing each implementation's auxiliary assumptions, since lexicalist grammars permit productive lexical rules \citep{mullerwechsler2014} while usage-based constructional networks can restrict head slots to conventionally entrenched items \citep{goldberg2019}. Testing how speakers extend subjectless accusative complementation and \mention{for V-ing} beyond established verbal and euphemistic models~-- including comparing non-verbal expressives like \mention{yay}/\mention{boo} and preposition-headed dismissals (\mention{to hell with X}, \mention{down with X})~-- remains a productive experimental agenda, but the corpus contribution stands independently: any viable grammar of English must encode both the shared subjectless NP/judgment-Reason profile and the sharp lexical, phraseological, and euphemistic asymmetries documented here.

\section{Limitations and conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ===========================================================================

Four methodological bounds should be kept in view. First, the TV Corpus compiles scripted television subtitles, where period drama boosts archaic British formulae (\mention{dash it}, \mention{damn and blast}, \mention{God rot}) and broadcast censorship increases euphemisms (\mention{frak}, \mention{eff}, \mention{fudge}); pairing distinct-series dispersion with spontaneous contemporary speech such as the Spoken BNC2014 \citep{loveEtAl2017SpokenBNC2014} would sharpen the boundary between living and period-bound heads. Second, GloWbE returned country tables without concordances during collection, leaving \texttt{G1} and \texttt{G2} as screened candidates and excluding context-free \mention{dash NP}. Third, Tier-1 and Tier-2 coding was conducted by a single coder; multi-coder reliability on a stratified sample would further strengthen borderline distinctions. Fourth, corpus frequencies measure attestation rather than graded speaker acceptability; targeted judgment studies~-- testing inanimate-target \mention{for V-ing} (\mention{Damn this printer for jamming again!}), \mention{to hell} vs.\ \mention{for V-ing}, and extension beyond established verbal and euphemistic heads~-- remain the natural next step.

Within those bounds, mapping 2.245~billion words across thirteen core heads and six \mention{fuck}-euphemisms resolves key empirical gaps in English grammar. English subjectless imprecatives and benedictives aren't unconditioned \mention{God}-optatives, primary interjections, or a lexically insensitive template. They form a structured family combining an open NP complement slot, non-reflexive second-person syntax, judgment-frame \mention{for V-ing} modification adopted even by secular \mention{fuck} and \mention{screw} (qualifying \citealp{quang1971}), sharp head- and expression-specific asymmetries (including civic vs.\ heart/soul \mention{bless}), and euphemistic distributional mirroring operating independently across both \mention{damn} and \mention{fuck}.
