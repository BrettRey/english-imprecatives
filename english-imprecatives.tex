\begin{abstract}
English subjectless bare-NP imprecatives and benedictives~-- such as \mention{Damn the bell!}, \mention{Fuck the doctors!}, \mention{Screw you for putting me in this position!}, \mention{Sod the choir!}, \mention{Bless his little cotton socks!}, and minced \mention{Darn my iPad!}~-- combine obligatory accusative pronouns and open lexical NPs with defective verbal syntax. Previous studies rely mostly on introspection over \mention{damn} and \mention{fuck}. This article maps thirteen core heads plus six \mention{fuck}-euphemisms across 19.95~million deduplicated words of locally audited British and American corpora (CABNC, BNC Baby XML, OANC) and 2.225~billion words of television subtitles and web English (TV Corpus, GloWbE, plus a COCA pilot). Five findings organize the paradigm. First, bare-NP heads separate cleanly from primary interjections (0/148 local and 0/76 TV bare-NP complements), require non-reflexive \mention{you} (\mention{Fuck you!} vs.\ imperative \mention{Go fuck yourself!}), and stand alongside productive bare-subjunctive dismissals (\mention{The law be damned!}). Second, revising \textcite[5]{quang1971}, bare-NP heads reject event-level adverbials while hosting evaluative \mention{for V-ing} reason PPs across six heads; controls show that \mention{for V-ing} is a general English evaluative adjunct (117 tokens across \mention{shame on}, \mention{good for/on}, \mention{kudos to}, and \mention{silly me}) restricted to accountable animate targets (13/13 lexical-NP tokens vs.\ 0/13 inanimate dismissals), in a one-way asymmetry with \mention{damn}-anchored \mention{to hell} (74:1 vs.\ 11:42 audited bare-NP KWICs, $p = 2.53 \times 10^{-22}$). Third, overt \mention{God}-optatives aren't only absent from secular heads but split sharply within \mention{bless} between civic targets (80/86, 93.0\% overt \mention{God}) and affective possessive idioms (12/272, 4.4\%; $p = 6.53 \times 10^{-59}$). Fourth, euphemistic lexical-variant inheritance replicates across both \mention{damn}-variants (\mention{darn}, \mention{dang}, maintaining a two-cell bare/participial paradigm with \mention{be darned/danged}) and \mention{fuck}-variants (\mention{frak}, \mention{feck}, \mention{eff}, \mention{frig}, \mention{fudge}, \mention{frick}: 61/100 audited bare-NP imprecatives). Finally, monolithic \mention{God}-ellipsis, flat constructionism, and primary-interjection accounts fail, whereas a modernized lexicalist defective-entry grammar and a structured constructional network converge on the corpus patterns and diverge chiefly on novel non-verbal heads.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} imprecatives, benedictives, construction grammar, defective verbs, English syntax

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ===========================================================================

English contains a recognizable, pragmatically vivid family of subjectless expressions in which an expressive head combines directly with a noun phrase and, optionally, a prepositional phrase. Attested instances span secular taboo maledictives, theological curses, affectionate benedictives, and mild minced oaths, as illustrated in (\ref{ex:intro-paradigm}).

\ea\label{ex:intro-paradigm}
\ea Damn the bell! \hfill (BNC Baby \texttt{H85})
\ex Fuck the doctors. \hfill (BNC Baby \texttt{CCW})
\ex Sod the choir! \hfill (BNC Baby \texttt{KPU})
\ex Bugger the rest! \hfill (CABNC \texttt{KB7})
\ex Screw the Cabinet and the rest. \hfill (OANC \texttt{Slate})
\ex Bless you for helping me out with the Watsons. \hfill (BNC Baby \texttt{G0Y})
\ex Darn you for raising us so well. \hfill (TV Corpus, \emph{Good Luck Charlie})
\z
\z

Since \textcite{quang1971} analysed \mention{fuck you} and \mention{damn him} as \enquote{English sentences without overt grammatical subject} headed by \enquote{quasi-verbs}, grammarians have struggled to place this family cleanly in English syntax. \textcite[923, 1360--1361]{huddleston2002} treat \mention{damn}, \mention{blast}, and \mention{fuck} in these frames as defective verbs or formulaic subjunctives bordering on interjections; \textcite[853]{quirk1985} group curses with minor clauses and formulaic exclamatives; formal semanticists model them as expressive or use-conditional items and profane quasi-verbs \citep{potts2007,gutzmann2015,gutzmann2019,portner2026}; and typological and corpus-pragmatic studies treat them as grammaticalized taboo terms or interactives \citep{mcenery2006,napolihoeksema2009,ljung2011,heine2023interactives}.

The empirical obstacle behind these diverging labels is straightforward. Almost every theoretical discussion has relied on introspection over two high-frequency heads~-- \mention{damn} and \mention{fuck}~-- without mapping how the full paradigm behaves in attested English. Looking at \mention{damn} or \mention{bless} invites a theological \mention{God}-ellipsis derivation; looking at \mention{fuck} or \mention{screw} invites an active defective-verb analysis; looking at frozen \mention{damn it} invites an interjection analysis. None of these local extrapolations has been evaluated systematically across the thirteen-head paradigm~-- theological \mention{damn}, \mention{bless}, \mention{curse}, \mention{blast}; secular taboo or violent \mention{fuck}, \mention{screw}, \mention{sod}, \mention{bugger}, \mention{hang}; and minced \mention{drat}, \mention{darn}, \mention{dash}, \mention{dang}~-- nor across parallel \mention{fuck}-euphemisms such as \mention{frak}, \mention{feck}, \mention{eff}, \mention{frig}, \mention{fudge}, and \mention{frick}.

A second obstacle is conceptual. Earlier accounts often treat four distinct analytical questions as a single binary choice between \enquote{defective verb} and \enquote{construction}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Syntactic status and boundaries:} How do bare-NP imprecatives and benedictives separate from primary interjections, ordinary imperatives, and source-sensitive secondary interjections?
\item \textbf{Lexical versus general properties:} Which extensions (\mention{God}-subjects, Goal PP \mention{to hell}, Reason PP \mention{for V-ing}, head coordination) generalize across heads, and which stay lexically anchored?
\item \textbf{NP-slot openness versus head-slot productivity:} Is the paradigm open in its complement slot, its head slot, or both~-- and how do minced oaths enter the head slot?
\item \textbf{Architectural adjudication:} Once the empirical facts are established, which theoretical frameworks are ruled out, and where do a modernized lexicalist defective-entry grammar and a structured constructional network converge or diverge?
\end{enumerate}

This article answers those four questions using a two-tier corpus design: 19.95~million deduplicated words of locally audited British and American corpora (CABNC, BNC Baby XML, OANC) paired with 2.225~billion words of contemporary television subtitles and web English (the TV Corpus, GloWbE, and an exploratory COCA pilot), including nine targeted follow-up queries testing evaluative adjunct controls, local \mention{God}-ellipsis within \mention{bless}, \mention{fuck}-euphemisms, and defective verbal paradigm cells. The evidence rules out monolithic \mention{God}-ellipsis, flat constructionism, and primary-interjection analyses, revises \textcite[5]{quang1971} on adverbial modification, documents euphemistic inheritance across two independent taboo families, and pinpoints the narrow empirical seam separating lexicalist and constructionist accounts.

\section{Grammatical profile, pragmatic subtypes, and analytical frameworks}
\label{sec:frameworks}

\subsection{Morphosyntactic diagnostics}
\label{sec:diagnostics}

The thirteen core heads fall into three historical strata: theological optatives (\mention{damn}, \mention{bless}, \mention{curse}, \mention{blast}), secular taboo or violent verbs (\mention{fuck}, \mention{screw}, \mention{sod}, \mention{bugger}, \mention{hang}), and minced oaths (\mention{drat}, \mention{darn}, \mention{dash}, \mention{dang}).\footnote{Historically, \mention{drat} contracts \mention{(Go)d rot}, \mention{darn} and \mention{dang} are phonological euphemisms for \mention{damn}, and \mention{dash} entered early nineteenth-century British English as a euphemism for \mention{damn} \citep{mcenery2006,gehweiler2008,ljung2011}.} Across these strata, the bare-NP frame exhibits seven interlocking grammatical properties, summarized in (\ref{ex:diagnostics}).

\ea\label{ex:diagnostics}
\ea Obligatory accusative pronoun form: Damn him! / Fuck them! / \ungram{Damn he!} / \ungram{Fuck they!}
\ex Open lexical NP complementation: Screw the \emph{Times}! / Sod the bar codes! / Darn my iPad!
\ex Non-reflexive 2sg object (unlike imperatives): Fuck you! / Damn you! vs.\ Go fuck yourself! (\ungram{Go fuck you!})
\ex Absence of tense, agreement, aspect, imperative \mention{do}-negation, and \mention{please}: \ungram{Damned the bell!} / \ungram{Don't damn him!} / \ungram{Please sod the choir!}
\ex Absence of ordinary overt subjects: \ungram{I sod the choir!} / \ungram{You screw the rules!} (non-imprecative)
\ex Rejection of eventive passives alongside productive bare-subjunctive dismissals: \ungram{The rules are being fucked (by me)!} vs.\ The law be damned! / Sony be buggered!
\ex Split modification: rejection of event-level verbal adverbials (\ungram{Fuck him quickly!}, \ungram{Damn him yesterday!}) alongside evaluative-act \mention{for V-ing} (\mention{Fuck her for killing herself!}) and lexically restricted \mention{to hell} (\mention{Damn him to hell!}).
\z
\z

Three grammatical refinements from (\ref{ex:diagnostics}) deserve immediate emphasis. First, accusative pronoun form in (\ref{ex:diagnostics}a) doesn't by itself prove transitive verbal Case assignment. English assigns \term{default accusative case} to pronouns outside nominative subject positions, including non-verbal evaluative exclamatives such as \mention{Poor me!}, \mention{Silly me!}, and \mention{Lucky him!} \citep{schutze2001}. What separates (\ref{ex:diagnostics}) from primary interjections like \mention{ouch}, \mention{oops}, \mention{ugh}, \mention{wow}, \mention{whoops}, and \mention{yuck} \citep{ameka1992,amekawilkins2006,meinard2015} is the combination of obligatory accusative pronominal objects (\ungram{Ouch him!}, \ungram{Ugh them!}), open determined/possessive lexical NPs integrated into a single syntactic unit, and evaluative \mention{for V-ing} modification.

Second, non-reflexive 2sg \mention{you} in (\ref{ex:diagnostics}c) cleanly distinguishes bare-NP imprecatives from ordinary imperatives \citep{quang1971}. Imperative clauses carry an implicit 2sg subject that binds a 2sg object into a reflexive (\mention{Go fuck yourself!}, \ungram{Go fuck you!}); bare-NP imprecatives have no 2sg subject and require non-reflexive \mention{you} (\mention{Fuck you!}, \mention{Screw you!}, \mention{Damn you!}).

Third, (\ref{ex:diagnostics}f--g) sharpen two classical claims. Bare-NP imprecatives don't undergo eventive passivization with a \mention{by}-agent, though English maintains a related bare-subjunctive dismissal construction \mention{X be damned/buggered!} (\mention{Everyone else be damned}, \mention{Arteries be damned}, Section~\ref{sec:minced}) alongside formulaic \mention{I'll be damned/darned/danged!}. More importantly, \textcite[5]{quang1971} claimed that English quasi-verb epithets \enquote{do not allow any adverbial elements at all}. As Sections~\ref{sec:tier1} and \ref{sec:dissoc} show, that claim holds for \emph{event-level} verbal modifiers (manner, instrument, temporal adverbs), while failing for \emph{evaluative-act} \mention{for V-ing} reason PPs.

\subsection{Four analytical positions}
\label{sec:four-positions}

Rather than pitting a single favoured theory against strawmen, I evaluate four explicit analytical positions from the literature, stating each~-- especially the lexicalist defective-verb position~-- in its strongest defensible form.

\paragraph{Position A: Uniform \mention{God}-optative ellipsis.}
Under a uniform ellipsis account, bare-NP imprecatives and benedictives are third-person subjunctive optatives with deleted subject \mention{God} (\mention{(God) bless you}, \mention{(God) damn him}). While historically real for \mention{bless} and \mention{damn} \citep{huddleston2002,ljung2011}, a synchronic ellipsis account predicts either productive overt \mention{God}-alternation across active heads or, locally on \mention{bless} and \mention{damn}, broadly parallel distributions between overt \mention{God}-optatives and bare-NP forms.

\paragraph{Position B: Primary or source-sensitive secondary interjections.}
Interjection theories distinguish \term{primary interjections} (\mention{ouch}, \mention{oops}, \mention{ugh}), which have no lexical homonym and don't take NP complements, from \term{secondary interjections} (\mention{damn!}, \mention{fuck!}, \mention{bless!}), which recruit lexical words into expressive exclamatives \citep{ameka1992,amekawilkins2006,meinard2015,heine2023interactives}. A pure primary-interjection treatment predicts zero syntactic complementation; a \emph{source-sensitive secondary-interjection} account allows bleached formulae (\mention{damn it}, \mention{dash it}, \mention{bless you}) to preserve frozen residues of their source clauses while still struggling if heads with no active finite transitive curse verb combine freely with open lexical NPs and participial frames.

\paragraph{Position C: Modernized lexicalist defective-verb / quasi-verb grammar.}
In the lexicalist tradition stretching from \textcite{quang1971} and \textcite{huddleston2002} to \textcite{mullerwechsler2014} and \textcite{portner2026}, each head carries a sense-specific defective lexical entry selecting an NP complement inside a subjectless clause (compare \textcite[7]{quang1971}'s phrase-structure rule \texttt{Epithet $\to$ Quasi-verb NP}). \textbf{Showing that a head lacks ordinary finite or past-tense forms doesn't refute a defective-verb analysis}: paradigm gaps are the defining property of defectiveness (compare English \mention{beware}, \mention{betide}, and \mention{methinks}). A strong lexicalist account can further incorporate (i) two-cell defective paradigms (bare stem plus past participle, as in \mention{darn} / \mention{be darned}), (ii) horizontal euphemistic lexical-variant links in the lexicon (\mention{damn} $\leftrightarrow$ \mention{darn/dang}, \mention{fuck} $\leftrightarrow$ \mention{frak/feck/eff/frig/fudge/frick}), (iii) English default accusative case \citep{schutze2001}, and (iv) general English evaluative \mention{for V-ing} adjuncts licensed outside the verb's own valence.

\paragraph{Position D: Usage-based structured constructional network.}
Under Construction Grammar \citep{fillmore1988mechanisms,goldberg1995constructions,goldberg2003,croft2001}, the pairing of an expressive head and its NP target constitutes a stored form--meaning pattern. A \emph{flat} constructional account predicts uniform productivity across all heads for every extension (\mention{God}-subjects, \mention{to hell}, \mention{for V-ing}, coordination). A \emph{structured} usage-based network \citep{bybee2010,diessel2019,goldberg2019,schmid2020} instead combines three layers: a schematic bare-NP expressive template \mention{[X\textsubscript{expr} NP]}, lexically anchored sub-constructions (civic \mention{God bless NP}, \mention{damn NP to hell}, British \mention{damn and blast}), and horizontal lexical-variant links across euphemisms~-- interacting with general English evaluative \mention{for V-ing} modification.

\section{Data and methods}
\label{sec:methods}
% ===========================================================================

No single corpus combines exhaustive, line-level manual verification over POS-tagged and spoken-segmented transcripts with the multi-billion-word scale needed to observe low-frequency frames such as minced oaths with lexical NPs, \mention{for V-ing} with lexical NPs, or \mention{fuck}-euphemisms. I pair two complementary tiers of corpus data to cover both.

\subsection{Tier 1: Locally audited British and American corpora (19.95M words)}
\label{sec:tier1-methods}

Tier~1 executes exhaustive extraction under explicit query rules across three local corpora:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{CABNC} \citep{albert2015cabnc}: 1,860 TalkBank CHAT files of spoken demographic British English from BNC1994 (246,783 utterances; 2,054,817 word tokens).
\item \textbf{BNC Baby XML} \citep{burnard2007bncbaby}: 182 CLAWS5 POS-tagged XML files spanning four registers~-- demographic spoken (\texttt{dem}), fiction (\texttt{fic}), newspaper (\texttt{news}), and academic prose (\texttt{aca})~-- totalling 4,001,787 tokens.
\item \textbf{OANC} \citep{ide2008oanc}: 8,824 plain-text files of American spoken (Switchboard, Charlotte) and written English (\emph{Slate}, \emph{Verbatim}, fiction, non-fiction, technical prose), totalling 14,449,791 word tokens.
\end{itemize}

Three methodological safeguards govern Tier~1. First, cross-checking BNC1994 speaker identifiers showed that 12 of BNC Baby's 30 \texttt{dem} files (\texttt{KB5}, \texttt{KB7}, \texttt{KBC}, \texttt{KBD}, \texttt{KBH}, \texttt{KBJ}, \texttt{KBP}, \texttt{KBW}, \texttt{KCC}, \texttt{KCF}, \texttt{KE2}, \texttt{KE4}; 559,070 tokens) duplicate CABNC. All pooled Tier-1 counts remove those 12 files from BNC Baby, yielding a strictly disjoint corpus of 10,854 files and \textbf{19,947,325 unique tokens} (5.50~million UK; 14.45~million US).

Second, inspecting 200 raw CABNC \texttt{.cha} files established that this TalkBank CHAT release contains \emph{no} millisecond Jeffersonian pause timings such as \texttt{(.)} or \texttt{(0.5)} (0/200 files). What it reliably preserves is sentence-unit segmentation inherited from BNC1994 \texttt{<s>} boundaries, visible as unit-initial capitalization inside multi-unit turns (e.g.\ \mention{Ouch My foot}, \mention{Fuck That would have been amusing}). I report these cues purely descriptively as \term{unit-boundary cues}~-- never as timed prosodic pauses~-- and avoid running inferential tests where boundary segmentation participates in distinguishing standalone interjections from complement-taking tokens.

Third, plain-text queries on CABNC and OANC target closed-class NP starters (\mention{it}, personal pronouns, demonstratives, articles, possessives, universal/indefinite pronouns), so they provide exhaustive retrieval \emph{under those query rules} rather than absolute recall over bare proper nouns. BNC Baby's CLAWS5 tags calibrate that gap directly: its extractor accepts \emph{any} immediate NP-starting tag~-- including proper nouns (\texttt{NP0}) and bare common nouns (\texttt{NN1}/\texttt{NN2})~-- whenever no sentence-ending punctuation intervenes.

Every Tier-1 candidate across all 13 target heads (596 deduplicated raw candidates) and all 6 primary-interjection comparators (\mention{ouch}, \mention{oops}, \mention{ugh}, \mention{wow}, \mention{whoops}, \mention{yuck}; 148 deduplicated raw candidates) was hand-adjudicated at line level in a reproducible Python script enforcing exact conservation ($\text{raw} = \text{bare-NP verified} + \text{God-optative} + \text{excluded}$).

\subsection{Tier 2: Large-scale subtitle and web corpora (2.225B words)}
\label{sec:tier2-methods}

Tier~2 scales the study to two Mark Davies corpora plus an exploratory COCA pilot \citep{davies2020coca} (every exact query string appears in Table~\ref{tab:appendix-queries}):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The TV Corpus} \citep{davies2019tv}: 325~million words compiled from British, Irish, American, Canadian, Australian, and New Zealand television \emph{subtitles} (1950--2018). Eleven baseline queries (\texttt{T1a}--\texttt{T8b}) map pronoun and lexical-NP complements, overt \mention{God}-optatives, Goal \mention{to hell} vs.\ Reason \mention{for V-ing}, coordination, and primary-interjection controls. Nine targeted follow-up queries (\texttt{F1a}--\texttt{F5c}) test non-imprecative evaluative \mention{for V-ing} controls, lexical-NP target animacy, six \mention{fuck}-euphemisms, within-\mention{bless} \mention{God}-ellipsis, minced \mention{God/Gosh} optatives, participial \mention{be + PART} cells, a positive-controlled finite/infinitival baseline, and all 65 KWICs of \mention{dash it}.
\item \textbf{GloWbE} \citep{davies2013glowbe,daviesfuchs2015}: 1.9~billion words of web English across 20 national varieties. Five queries (\texttt{G1}--\texttt{G4}) probe minced oaths (\mention{drat}, \mention{darn}, \mention{dash}, \mention{dang}) in lexical-NP, accusative-pronoun, attributive/adverbial mirroring, and past-tense transitive frames. \end{itemize}

\subsection{Coding taxonomy, exclusions, and statistical protocol}
\label{sec:coding}

Verified Tier-1 bare-NP tokens fall into seven mutually exclusive formal categories: fused \mention{it}-formulae (\mention{damn it}, \mention{fuck it}, \mention{sod it}); routine \mention{bless you} (sneeze, parting, and gratitude, including \mention{Bless you for helping me out with the Watsons}); 2sg \mention{you} (\mention{fuck you}, \mention{damn you}); unambiguous non-2sg accusative pronouns (\mention{fuck me}, \mention{bugger him}, \mention{bless her}); bare demonstratives (\mention{sod this}, \mention{bugger that}); possessive body-part/soul formulae (\mention{bless his heart}, \mention{bless his cotton socks}, \mention{damn your eyes}); and open full lexical NPs (\mention{Damn the bell!}, \mention{Screw the Cabinet}). Overt \mention{God + HEAD + NP} optatives are tallied separately so they never contaminate bare-NP counts.

Strict exclusions remove: literal or finite transitive verbs (\mention{to hang the curtains}, \mention{won't screw it up}, \mention{to darn her own stockings}); negative quantifier idioms (\mention{bugger all}, \mention{sod all}, \mention{fuck all}); standalone interjections followed by a new clause (\mention{Fuck. That would have been amusing}); metalinguistic mentions and dictionary glosses (such as \emph{Verbatim} citations of \mention{Curse it!} or \mention{Drat you and your dog!}); proper-noun homonyms (e.g.\ CABNC \mention{Very pretty, Dash, that}, referring to the UK clothing retailer \emph{Dash}); and West African English ditransitive \mention{dash} (\enquote{give a gift/tip}) in GloWbE.

Inferential tests use exact two-sided Fisher's exact tests strictly on matched-denominator contingency tables, paired with document/file dispersion in Tier~1 and distinct television-series dispersion in Tier~2 so repeated lines within a single file or series can't masquerade as independent grammatical productivity.

\subsection{Separation from primary interjections and CABNC unit boundaries}
\label{sec:cabnc-boundaries}

Across all 19.95~million deduplicated Tier-1 words, the six primary-interjection comparators (\mention{ouch}, \mention{oops}, \mention{ugh}, \mention{wow}, \mention{whoops}, \mention{yuck}) produce 148 raw \mention{PRIMARY + NP-starter} sequences (23 CABNC, 16 deduplicated BNC Baby, 109 OANC). My foot}; \mention{Ugh, the prep}).

CABNC's BNC1994 \texttt{<s>} unit-boundary capitalization cues align with this categorical split. Across all 80 verified bare-NP tokens in CABNC (dispersed across 64 distinct \texttt{.cha} files), 0/80 carry an intervening unit-boundary cue between the head and its NP. \subsection{BNC Baby POS calibration, regional splits, and Tier-1 \mentionhead{God}-optatives}
\label{sec:tier1-regional}

BNC Baby's CLAWS5 scan calibrates what closed-class \mention{PRON/DET} queries miss. Accepting \texttt{NP0} and bare common nouns recovered three genuine bare-NP tokens~-- \mention{Damn McIllvanney} (\texttt{CCW:483}, \texttt{NP0}), \mention{Oh, screw Jesus Christ} (\texttt{HR9:2136}, \texttt{NP0}), and bedtime prayer \mention{night, bless mummy and dad} (\texttt{KD3:2485}, \texttt{NN1})~-- while exposing an important diagnostic ambiguity: \mention{Damn DTs.} (\texttt{GVL:2716}, \texttt{NP0}) can be parsed either as a bare-NP imprecative or as a verbless NP with prenominal \mention{damn} (compare \mention{Damn rubbish.}, \texttt{CFY:593}, and \mention{Damn nuisance.}, \texttt{CFY:992}). \subsection{What the evidence rules out}
\label{sec:ruled-out}

Three monolithic accounts fail against Table~\ref{tab:verdict}. First, \textbf{uniform \mention{God}-optative ellipsis (Position~A)} fails both across the paradigm (only 1 non-\mention{it} secular token in 325~million words) and locally within \mention{bless}, where civic \mention{God bless} (93.0\% overt \mention{God}) and affective bare \mention{bless} (95.6\% bare) form distinct sub-constructions.

Second, \textbf{flat constructionism (flat Position~D)} fails on every lexical asymmetry: \mention{to hell} stays anchored to \mention{damn}/\mention{dang}, coordination stays frozen on British \mention{damn and blast}, overt \mention{God} stays restricted to theological heads and frozen \mention{it}-euphemisms, and regional micro-constructions remain sharply partitioned.

Third, \textbf{primary-interjection accounts (primary Position~B)} fail categorically (0/148 local and 0/76 TV bare-NP complements). A \emph{source-sensitive secondary-interjection} account \citep{amekawilkins2006,heine2023interactives} fits bleached \mention{it}-formulae (\mention{damn it}, \mention{dash it}, \mention{gosh darn it}) and routine \mention{bless you}, but once heads take open lexical NPs (\mention{Screw the committee}, \mention{Darn my iPad}, \mention{Frak the Gods}), accountable-target \mention{for V-ing}, or participial \mention{be darned/danged}, their syntax outstrips interjectional freezing.

\subsection{NP-slot openness versus head-slot productivity}
\label{sec:productivity}

Separating \textbf{NP-slot openness} from \textbf{head-slot productivity} clarifies what's productive~-- and what isn't. The \textbf{complement NP slot} is robustly open across every living head, attesting thousands of novel definite, demonstrative, possessive, coordinated, and proper-name NPs. The \textbf{head slot}, by contrast, rejects primary interjections across 345~million audited words and expands along two constrained pathways: (i) \textbf{taboo and expressive verbal recruitment} (\mention{fuck}, \mention{screw}, \mention{sod}, \mention{bugger}, \mention{damn}, \mention{bless}; \citealp{mcenery2006,napolihoeksema2009,ljung2011}), and (ii) \textbf{horizontal euphemistic lexical-variant inheritance} (\mention{darn/dang} from \mention{damn}; \mention{frak/feck/eff/frig/fudge/frick} from \mention{fuck}), copying a source lexeme's multi-constructional distribution~-- bare-NP imprecatives, prenominal/adverbial slots, participial \mention{be darned/danged}, and phrasal verbs (\mention{don't eff this up})~-- without developing ordinary finite curse inflection.

\subsection{Lexicalist defective entries versus a structured constructional network}
\label{sec:convergence}

The central theoretical lesson is that \textbf{observational corpus data underdetermine the choice between Position~C (a modernized lexicalist defective-entry grammar) and structured Position~D (a usage-based constructional network)}. Under \textbf{Position~C} \citep{quang1971,huddleston2002,mullerwechsler2014,portner2026}, each living head carries a defective lexical entry restricted to a subjectless expressive clause (cf.\ \textcite[7]{quang1971}'s \texttt{Epithet $\to$ Quasi-verb NP}). Missing finite forms (\texttt{G4}, \texttt{F5b}) follow from lexical defectiveness; \mention{be darned/danged} (\texttt{F5a}) reflect a two-cell paradigm mirrored from \mention{damn}; accusative pronouns receive English default case \citep{schutze2001}; \mention{for V-ing} (\texttt{T7b}, \texttt{F1a--b}, \texttt{F2}) is a general English evaluative adjunct; and all lexical asymmetries and horizontal euphemism links live in the lexicon.

Under \textbf{structured Position~D} \citep{goldberg2003,goldberg2019,croft2001,bybee2010,diessel2019,schmid2020}, speakers generalize a schematic expressive construction \mention{[X\textsubscript{expr} NP]} linked vertically to lexically anchored sub-constructions (civic \mention{God bless NP}, affective \mention{bless POSS heart}, \mention{damn NP to hell}, British \mention{damn and blast}) and horizontally to euphemistic allolexes (\mention{darn/dang} $\leftrightarrow$ \mention{damn}; \mention{frak/feck/eff/frig/fudge/frick} $\leftrightarrow$ \mention{fuck}), combining compositionally with general evaluative \mention{for V-ing}.

These two architectures diverge on \textbf{schema-driven coercion of novel non-verbal heads lacking both a transitive verbal source and a taboo allolex model}. If speakers coerce a freshly coined standalone exclamation~-- with no verbal uses and no link to \mention{damn} or \mention{fuck}~-- directly into \mention{[X\textsubscript{nonce} him / the rules]} with accusative case and accountable-target \mention{for V-ing}, that favours an autonomous constructional schema (Position~D). If novel heads resist bare-NP complementation unless licensed as transitive verbs or explicit euphemisms for an existing head, that favours Position~C's lexicalist architecture (or an item-anchored network).

\section{Limitations and conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ===========================================================================

Three bounds apply. First, the TV Corpus compiles television subtitles, where period drama boosts archaic British formulae (\mention{dash it}, \mention{damn and blast}, \mention{God rot}); distinct-series dispersion and genre audits (\texttt{F5c}, \texttt{T8a}) track this skew without removing differences between scripted dialogue and spontaneous speech. Second, GloWbE returned country tables without concordances, making \texttt{G1} and \texttt{G2} lexically screened candidate attestations and leaving residual British \mention{dash NP} for future concordance work. Third, corpus counts measure attestation rather than graded acceptability; experimental judgement studies testing both the \mention{to hell} vs.\ \mention{for V-ing} asymmetry and nonce-head coercion remain the natural next step.

Even within those bounds, 2.24~billion words across thirteen core heads and six \mention{fuck}-euphemisms clarify the grammar of the paradigm. English bare-NP imprecatives and benedictives aren't deleted \mention{God}-optatives, primary interjections, or a flat schema. They form a structured family combining an open NP slot, non-reflexive 2sg syntax, general evaluative \mention{for V-ing} modification (revising \textcite{quang1971}), sharp head- and sub-sense-specific asymmetries (including civic vs.\ affective \mention{bless}), and horizontal euphemistic inheritance operating independently across both \mention{damn} and \mention{fuck}.
